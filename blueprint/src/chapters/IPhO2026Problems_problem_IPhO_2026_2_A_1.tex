reflections.  The positive threshold x\_N is the largest distance from the
optical axis for which a ray undergoes at most N reflections.  Use Figures
2c--2e for the mirror and limiting-ray geometry.

Current subquestion:
Find the general expression for the threshold x\_N in terms of R and the positive integer N.

\subsection*{Derivation bridges}

\begin{theorem}[The limiting ray undergoes exactly $N + 1$ reflections]
\label{thm:IPhO2026Problems_problem_IPhO_2026_2_A_1:limiting_ray_reflection_count}
\lean{IPhO2026_2_A_1.HalfCylindricalMirror.limiting_ray_reflection_count}
\uses{def:IPhO2026Problems_problem_IPhO_2026_2_A_1:HalfCylindricalMirror}
For the ray whose first impact has polar angle $\alpha = \pi/(2N+1)$ across
the threshold ($N = n + 1$ in $0$-based indexing), the impact points are the
odd multiples $\pi/(2N+1), 3\pi/(2N+1), \dots, (2N+1)\pi/(2N+1) = \pi$, so
its $(N+1)$-st impact lands exactly on the rim and
$N_{\mathrm{refl}}\bigl(R\cos(\pi/(2N+1))\bigr) = N + 1$ ($n + 2$, $0$-based).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the bundled reflection-count law, $N_{\mathrm{refl}}(R\cos\alpha)$ counts
the odd multiples of $\alpha = \pi/(2N+1)$ not exceeding $\pi$; these are
exactly $(2k+1)\pi/(2N+1)$ for $0 \le k \le N$, i.e.\ $N + 1$ impacts, the
last being the rim impact, which counts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The two recorded forms agree]
\label{thm:IPhO2026Problems_problem_IPhO_2026_2_A_1:threshold_forms_agree}
\lean{IPhO2026_2_A_1.HalfCylindricalMirror.threshold_forms_agree}
\uses{def:IPhO2026Problems_problem_IPhO_2026_2_A_1:HalfCylindricalMirror}
Cosine--sine bridge (recorded answer, both forms conclusion-side): for the
positive integer $N$ ($N = n + 1$, $0$-based),
\[
R\sin\!\frac{(2N-1)\pi}{4N+2} \;=\; R\cos\!\frac{\pi}{2N+1} .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pure trigonometry: $(2N-1)\pi/(4N+2) = \pi/2 - \pi/(2N+1)$ and
$\sin(\pi/2 - \theta) = \cos\theta$.
\end{proof}
