# Binary character foundation for original M6

This module formalizes the finite binary character argument in sections 4 and 5 of the frozen original M6 proof. `Vector m` is `Fin m → ZMod 2`; the scalar product is the sum of coordinate products. The integer character is `(-1)^dot.val`. A binary subspace is an actual `Submodule`, and its finite set of words is obtained by filtering all vectors by subspace membership.

The first eight frozen targets prove binary sign identities, character multiplication under addition, the coordinate-product identity, subspace orthogonality by translation, and the general weighted MacWilliams identity. Its weights are arbitrary coordinate functions with values in `Polynomial ℤ`. The formula uses multiplication by the integer subspace cardinality, so it is an exact integral polynomial identity and needs no rational rounding convention. Orthogonality and the transform formula are theorem conclusions, never assumptions.

The planned pinned specialization imports the frozen definitions from `M6.Pinned`: Option-valued pins, agreement, nonzero-coordinate cardinality, and the polynomial enumerator. Each coordinate boundary factor is zero when its bit violates its pin and otherwise is `X^bit.val`; its character factor is the two-bit signed sum of these boundary factors. The coordinate-product bridge and the general weighted theorem will then yield the precise pinned dual enumerator in the same representation used by witness recovery.

No cyclic-code, squarefreeness, transfer-algorithm or performance premise is needed for this general module. The cyclic specialization and transfer implementation are handled by their owners. The work follows the original M6 scope and does not introduce a stronger acceptance gate.

The isolated project uses the existing Lean and Mathlib pin. Initial preflight instance repair is preserved under `preflight_type_repair/`; it supplies the classical decision procedure inside a named conditional indicator and changes no mathematics. Model experiments use the existing harness, Mathlib and Physlib retrieval, `gpt-6-astra` with medium effort, concurrency 16 and at most five rounds.
