\chapter{Generated finite microscopic-dynamics acceptance target}
\label{ch:ArchonPhysics_Generated_problem_microscopic_dynamics}

This consumer checks that the local \texttt{ArchonPhysics} dependency exposes
one coherent finite-dimensional interface: a $C^1$ microscopic vector field,
a unique local solution germ through each initial state, and conditional
energy--momentum conservation on any segment on which the concrete ODE is
already known to hold.  It asserts no global existence, random-disorder or
thermodynamic limit, kinetic limit, equilibration, or thermalization law.

\begin{definition}\leanok
[Unique local solution-germ property]
\label{def:microscopic-consumer:unique-local-solution-germ}
\lean{ArchonPhysics.Generated.MicroscopicDynamics.HasUniqueLocalSolutionGerm}
\uses{def:microscopic:phase-space, def:microscopic:vector-field}
For fixed $m,n,\lambda$, this property says that every initial pair $(t_0,z_0)$
admits $\varepsilon>0$ and a path solving the microscopic vector ODE in the
within-derivative sense on
$[t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon]$.  Any other path with the same initial
value and the ordinary derivative equation on the open interval agrees with
it as a germ at $t_0$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}

\leanok
This definition restates exactly the quantified local existence and eventual
uniqueness interface expected from the generated dependency.  It contains no
claim of continuation beyond the local interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\leanok
[Finite microscopic-dynamics formalization target]
\label{thm:physics:microscopic_dynamics:target}
\lean{ArchonPhysics.Generated.MicroscopicDynamics.microscopic_dynamics_physics_formalization_target}
\uses{def:microscopic-consumer:unique-local-solution-germ,
      thm:microscopic:vector-field-contdiff,
      thm:microscopic:local-solution-germ,
      thm:microscopic:energy-endpoints,
      thm:microscopic:momentum-endpoints}
For a nonempty finite periodic chain and $1\le n$, the microscopic vector field
is $C^1$ and has the unique local solution-germ property.  Moreover, for every
path $z$ and endpoints $s,t$, if the concrete ODE holds at every point of
$\operatorname{uIcc}(s,t)$, then both Hamiltonian energy and total momentum
agree at $s$ and $t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\leanok
Use continuous differentiability and local Picard--Lindelof existence from the
microscopic-dynamics dependency.  Under the separately supplied whole-segment
ODE hypothesis, apply its conditional endpoint conservation theorems for
energy and momentum.  Neither this hypothesis nor the local solution theorem
provides a global trajectory, a kinetic limit, or thermalization.
\end{proof}
