\#\# T2. Kinetics of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky Reaction
T2. Kinetics of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction 6\% When the chemicals KBrO3, CH2(COOH)2 (malonic acid, – MA), Ce(SO4)2, and H2SO4 are mixed, the concentration, 𝐶, of Ce species and the colour of the solution oscillates between yellow (due to Ce4+) and colourless (due to Ce3+). A schematic picture is given below: 2.1 Write the overall balanced reaction equation of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction (BZ reaction). Note: in this reaction, MA is oxidised to CO2, BrO− 3 is reduced to Br−, and Ce(IV) is a catalyst. The mechanism of the BZ reaction is shown below (BMA −CHBr(COOH)2): Process A ⎧ ⎪ ⎪ ⎨ ⎪ ⎪ ⎩ (1) HBrO2 + BrO− 3 + H+ 𝑘1 −−→2BrO⋅ 2 + H2O (2) BrO⋅ 2 + Ce3+ + H+ 𝑘2 −−→HBrO2 + Ce4+ (3) 2HBrO2 𝑘3 −−→BrO− 3 + HBrO + H+ 𝑘1 = 1.0 × 104 M−2 s−1 𝑘2 = 6.2 × 104 M−2 s−1 𝑘3 = 4.0 × 107 M−1 s−1 Process B ⎧ ⎪ ⎪ ⎨ ⎪ ⎪ ⎩ (4) HBrO2 + Br−+ H+ 𝑘4 −−→2HBrO (5) BrO− 3 + Br−+ 2H+ 𝑘5 −−→HBrO + HBrO2 (6) HBrO + MA 𝑘6 −−→BMA + H2O 𝑘4 = 2.0 × 109 M−2 s−1 𝑘5 = 2.1 M−3 s−1 𝑘6 = 8.2 M−1 s−1 Process C \{(7) Ce4+ + BMA 𝑘7 −−→Ce3+ + Br−+ other products 𝑘7 = 1.0 × 102 M−1 s−1 In the BZ reaction, Process C occurs continuously, while Processes A and B alternate with each other. Assume that: - when Process A occurs the solution is yellow and Process B practically does not occur; - when Process B occurs the solution is colourless and Process A practically does not occur; - [BrO3−]0 = 0.06 M, [MA]0 = 0.1 M, [H+]0 = 0.8 M, [Ce4+]0 = 0.001 M; - the concentrations of the reactants (except Ce4+) and pH are maintained constant throughout the BZ reaction.

\begin{theorem}\leanok
[Process-A stationary balance]
\label{lem:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:process_a_balance}
\lean{IChO2026Problems.T2A2.processA_stationary_balance}
\uses{def:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:data}
For $x_A=[\mathrm{HBrO_2}]_A$, $r=[\mathrm{BrO_2^{\bullet}}]$, and
$c=[\mathrm{Ce^{3+}}]$, assume the two stationary rate equations
\[
  2k_1x_Abh-k_2rch=0,
  \qquad -k_1x_Abh+k_2rch-2k_3x_A^2=0.
\]
Then $k_1x_Abh-2k_3x_A^2=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\leanok
Add the two displayed equalities.  The $k_2rch$ terms cancel and the
coefficient $2-1$ of $k_1x_Abh$ leaves precisely the claimed balance.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\leanok
[Process-A stationary concentration]
\label{lem:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:process_a_concentration}
\lean{IChO2026Problems.T2A2.processA_hbro2_concentration}
\uses{def:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:data, def:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:matches_supplied_data, lem:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:process_a_balance}
If $x_A>0$, then the Process-A balance gives
\[
 x_A=\frac{k_1}{2k_3}bh.
\]
With the source values this is $6.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{M}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\leanok
Factor the balance as $x_A(k_1bh-2k_3x_A)=0$.  Positivity excludes the
zero factor, so division by the positive $2k_3$ yields the formula.
Substitution gives $(10^4/(2\cdot4\cdot10^7))\cdot0.06\cdot0.8
=6.0\cdot10^{-6}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\leanok
[Process-B stationary concentration]
\label{lem:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:process_b_concentration}
\lean{IChO2026Problems.T2A2.processB_hbro2_concentration}
\uses{def:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:data, def:chem:icho_2026_t2_a2:matches_supplied_data}
For $x_B=[\mathrm{HBrO_2}]_B$ and $y=[\mathrm{Br^-}]$, assume $y>0$ and
the Process-B stationary equation
\[
 -k_4x_Byh+k_5byh^2=0.
\]
Then $x_B=(k_5/k_4)bh$, hence $x_B=5.04\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{M}$ for
the source data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\leanok
Factor the equation as $yh(-k_4x_B+k_5bh)=0$.  The maintained acid
concentration and $y$ are positive, so the second factor is zero.  Divide by
$k_4>0$ and substitute $(2.1/(2\cdot10^9))\cdot0.06\cdot0.8$.
\end{proof}
