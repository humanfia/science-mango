\begin{lemma}[Isotope-ratio equation and its real solution]
\label{lem:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:ratio-equation}
\lean{IChO2026.T1.A3.relativeIntensityM1_eq, IChO2026.T1.A3.carbonCountEstimate_spec, IChO2026.T1.A3.carbonCountEstimate_near_ten}
\leanok
Under the two-isotope model the relative $[M+1]^+$ intensity of $n$ carbons
simplifies to $n\,p_{13}/p_{12}$ (Equation (1) of the marking scheme, cleared
of denominators).  At the measured ratio $1/9$ this equation holds iff
$x = p_{12}/(9\,p_{13})$, and that real solution $989/99 \approx 9.99$ lies
within one half of $10$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Write $I([M+1]^+)/I([M]^+) = \binom{n}{1} p_{12}^{n-1} p_{13} / p_{12}^n$;
using $\binom{n}{1} = n$ and $p_{12}^n = p_{12}^{n-1} p_{12}$ (the case
$n = 0$ vanishing on both sides) the powers cancel and the ratio is
$n\,p_{13}/p_{12}$.  Solving $x\,p_{13}/p_{12} = 1/9$ for $x$ and clearing
denominators gives $x = p_{12}/(9\,p_{13})$; numerically
$0.989/(9\cdot 0.011) = 989/99$, and $|989/99 - 10| = 1/99 < 1/2$.
In Lean the cancellation is \texttt{field\_simp} with the positivity side
conditions, and the estimates are \texttt{norm\_num}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Determination of the carbon number of W]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:carbon-count}
\lean{IChO2026.T1.A3.carbon_count_eq_ten}
\uses{lem:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:ratio-equation}
\leanok
Any whole number $n$ of carbon atoms within half a unit of the
mass-spectrometric estimate $p_{12}/(9\,p_{13})$ equals $10$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The triangle inequality gives
$|n - 10| \le |n - \hat n| + |\hat n - 10| < 1/2 + 1/2 = 1$, where
$\hat n = p_{12}/(9\,p_{13})$; the only natural number with $|n - 10| < 1$ is
$n = 10$ (\texttt{omega} after casting back to $\mathbb{N}$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Identification of W: subquestion 1.3]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:target}
\lean{IChO2026.T1.A3.hasPhenol_iff, IChO2026.T1.A3.W_identification, IChO2026.T1.A3.subquestion_1_3}
\uses{thm:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:carbon-count, def:physics:icho_2026_t1_a3:setup}
\leanok
If $w$ gives the characteristic colour change with aqueous Fe${}^{3+}$, and
the characteristic-test law forces every such compound to be phenolic, and the
mass-spectrometric estimate pins the carbon count of $w$ to within half an
atom, then $w$ has $10$ carbon atoms and $w$ is compound 5, eugenol.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The carbon number $10$ is the determination above.  The test law makes $w$
phenolic, and among the ten candidates only compound 1 (zingerone,
$\mathrm{C}_{11}\mathrm{H}_{14}\mathrm{O}_3$) and compound 5 (eugenol,
$\mathrm{C}_{10}\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_2$) are phenolic; the zingerone
alternative contradicts the carbon count $11 \ne 10$, so $w$ is eugenol.  In
Lean the case split is \texttt{rcases} over \texttt{hasPhenol\_iff} and the
impossible branch is closed by evaluating \texttt{Compound.formula}.
\end{proof}
