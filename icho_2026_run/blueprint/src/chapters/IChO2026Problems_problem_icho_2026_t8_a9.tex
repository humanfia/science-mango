\begin{definition}[Excited-state channels, first-order decay, and the quenched fraction]
\label{def:physics:icho_2026_t8_a9:setup}
\lean{IChO2026.T8.A9.S1Channels, IChO2026.T8.A9.T1Channels, IChO2026.T8.A9.FirstOrderDecay, IChO2026.T8.A9.intrinsicDecayS1, IChO2026.T8.A9.effectiveDecayS1, IChO2026.T8.A9.intrinsicDecayT1, IChO2026.T8.A9.quenchedFraction, IChO2026.T8.A9.quenchingConstant, IChO2026.T8.A9.tau0, IChO2026.T8.A9.redConcentration, IChO2026.T8.A9.quenchingPercent}
\leanok
The S1 state decays unimolecularly through fluorescence ($kF$), internal
conversion ($kIC$), and intersystem crossing ($kISC$), and the T1 state
through phosphorescence ($kP$), non-radiative decay ($kN$), and reverse
intersystem crossing ($kRISC$); a first-order decay law for a concentration
readout $c$ with constant $k_0$ is $\dot c = -k_0 c$.  The quenched fraction
of an excited state with intrinsic first-order constant $k_0$, bimolecular
quenching constant $k_q$, and reductant concentration $Q$ is
$k_q Q / (k_0 + k_q Q)$.  The source readouts are $k_S = 2.7\times10^{9}$,
$k_T = 1.5\times10^{8}$ $\mathrm{M^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, $\tau_0(S1) = 2.9$ ns,
$\tau_0(T1) = 84$ $\mu$s, and $[\mathrm{Red}] = 0.1$ M; the quenching
percentage is $100$ times the quenched fraction with $k_0 = \tau_0^{-1}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[First-order decay trajectory and lifetime]
\label{lem:physics:icho_2026_t8_a9:trajectory}
\lean{IChO2026.T8.A9.FirstOrderDecay_trajectory, IChO2026.T8.A9.FirstOrderDecay_at_lifetime}
\leanok
A first-order decay $\dot c = -k_0 c$ has the exponential trajectory
$c(t) = c(0) e^{-k_0 t}$: the product $c(t) e^{k_0 t}$ has zero derivative
everywhere, hence is constant.  In particular, at the unquenched emission
lifetime $t = \tau_0 = k_0^{-1}$ the concentration has fallen to
$c(0) e^{-1}$, the formula for the lifetime of a reagent in a first-order
reaction invoked by the marking scheme.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Ratio-of-rates and Stern--Volmer forms of the quenched fraction]
\label{lem:physics:icho_2026_t8_a9:sternvolmer}
\lean{IChO2026.T8.A9.quenchedFraction_eq_ratio, IChO2026.T8.A9.quenchedFraction_sternVolmer, IChO2026.T8.A9.eq_8_10_3, IChO2026.T8.A9.eq_8_10_4}
\leanok
For $c \neq 0$ the ratio-of-rates form is independent of the excited-state
concentration,
$\frac{k_q c Q}{k_0 c + k_q c Q} = \frac{k_q Q}{k_0 + k_q Q}$, and with
$\tau_0 = k_0^{-1}$ it takes the Stern--Volmer form
$\frac{k_q \tau_0 Q}{1 + k_q \tau_0 Q}$; both identities are plain fraction
arithmetic, the first cancelling $c$ and the second cancelling $k_0^{-1}$.
Specializing to $k_0 = kF + kIC$ (the $kF \gg kISC$ approximation) gives the
recorded eq.~8.10.3, and $k_0 = kP + kN + kRISC$ gives eq.~8.10.4.
\end{lemma}
