\paragraph{Current subquestion.}
Tick one correct statement regarding the value of n (number of type a fragments). (a) n is an even number, (b) n is an odd number, (c) n can be either an odd or an even number.

\paragraph{Recorded answer/context.}
Each bond involves two atoms, so the total number of atoms with broken bonds (indicated by the wavy line on the fragments of the molecule) must be an even number. For fragments with a known number of repeats in the PL1 molecule, the total number of atoms with broken bonds is 17 (2·2 + 3·3 + 4). Thus the total number of hydrogen atoms with broken bonds must be an odd number. Therefore, n is an odd number. Correct answer: 1 point. 1.0 pt

\paragraph{Formalization target.}
The fragment census is the inductive type
\texttt{FragmentKind} with one broken bond on a (hydrogen), two on b
(phosphorus), three on c (oxygen) and one on d (carbonyl carbon), in
quantities $n, 2, 3, 4$ (\texttt{pl1Copies}); an \texttt{Assembly} is a
fixed-point-free involution \texttt{bond} on the terminus pool
\texttt{OpenEnd n} that pairs no two oxygen-borne termini
(\texttt{no\_peroxide}).

\begin{lemma}[Pool census]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t5_a1:card_openEnd}
\lean{IChO2026.T5.A1.card_openEnd}
\leanok
The number of broken-bond termini in the fragment pool with $n$ type-a
fragments is $\sum_k \mathrm{copies}(k)\cdot\mathrm{openEnds}(k)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{Fintype.card\_sigma} turns the $\Sigma$-type of termini into
the sum over fragment kinds; each factor has cardinality
$\mathrm{copies}(k)\cdot\mathrm{openEnds}(k)$ by
\texttt{Fintype.card\_prod} and \texttt{Fintype.card\_fin}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Handshaking: the terminus pool is even]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t5_a1:even_totalOpenEnds_of_assembly}
\lean{IChO2026.T5.A1.even_totalOpenEnds_of_assembly}
\leanok
In any assembly the total number of atoms with broken bonds is even:
each bond involves exactly two of them.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Pair each terminus $e$ with its bond partner as the unordered pair
$\mathrm{s}(e,\mathrm{bond}\,e) \in \mathrm{Sym2}$.  Every fibre of this
pairing map is exactly the two-element set $\{e, \mathrm{bond}\,e\}$ ---
by \texttt{Sym2.eq\_iff}, membership forces $x = e$ or
$x = \mathrm{bond}\,e$, involutivity shows both are indeed mapped there,
and fixed-point-freeness keeps them distinct --- so the cardinality of
the pool is twice the number of pairs
(\texttt{Finset.card\_eq\_sum\_card\_image},
\texttt{Finset.card\_pair}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The known fragments contribute $17$]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t5_a1:totalOpenEnds_eq}
\lean{IChO2026.T5.A1.totalOpenEnds_eq}
\leanok
The pool total is $n + (2\cdot 2 + 3\cdot 3 + 4) = n + 17$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Enumerate the four-element \texttt{Finset.univ} of fragment kinds and
evaluate each summand; simp's numeral arithmetic closes
$n\cdot 1 + (2\cdot 2 + 3\cdot 3 + 4\cdot 1) = n + 17$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Subquestion 5.1: option (b) is correct]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t5_a1:subquestion_5_1}
\lean{IChO2026.T5.A1.subquestion_5_1}
\leanok
If the fragment pool with $n$ type-a fragments assembles into the
non-ionised form of PL1, then $n$ is odd.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
By the two counting principles above, $n + 17$ is even, say
$n + 17 = r + r$; then $r \geq 9$ and $n = 2(r - 9) + 1$ is odd
(\texttt{omega}).
\end{proof}
