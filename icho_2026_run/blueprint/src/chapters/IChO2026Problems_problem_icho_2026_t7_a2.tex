\begin{definition}[Flow-sheet balance of the Navoiazot ammonia plant]
\label{def:physics:icho_2026_t7_a2:balance}
\lean{IChO2026.T7.A2.AmmoniaPlantBalance, IChO2026.T7.A2.FlowSheetBalance}

The annual mole/mass bookkeeping of the Fig.~1 flow sheet records the ammonia
output (tons), the overall yield, the molar masses of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ and
$\mathrm{CH_4}$, and the annual molar amounts of every stream species.  The
balance predicate imposes the unit conversions $n = m/M$ and
$m = n \cdot M$ (with $1$ ton $= 10^6$ g), the quantitative stoichiometry of
R1 ($\mathrm{CH_4 + H_2O \to CO + 3H_2}$), R2
($\mathrm{2CH_4 + O_2 \to 2CO + 4H_2}$, $\mathrm{O_2}$ fully consumed) and R3
($\mathrm{CO + H_2O \to CO_2 + H_2}$, all CO shifted), the stoichiometric

$n(\mathrm{N_2}) = 4\,n(\mathrm{O_2})$, the overall-yield relation
$n(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 2 \cdot \mathrm{yield} \cdot n(\mathrm{N_2})$ of R4, the
hydrogen and methane front-end balances, and the physical positivity and
nonnegativity side conditions.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Annual methane demand of the Navoiazot plant]
\label{thm:physics:icho_2026_t7_a2:target}
\lean{IChO2026.T7.A2.icho_2026_t7_a2}
\leanok
For a plant satisfying the Fig.~1 flow-sheet balances, producing
$660\,000$ tons of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ per year at a $97.0\,\%$ overall yield,
with $M(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 17$ g/mol and $M(\mathrm{CH_4}) = 16$ g/mol, the
annual methane demand satisfies the mole-ratio bridge
$n(\mathrm{CH_4}) = \tfrac{7}{8}\,n(\mathrm{N_2})$ and its mass is the
recorded answer $280\,000$ tons to the nearest $1000$ tons, i.e.\
$|m(\mathrm{CH_4}) - 280\,000| \le 500$ tons.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The amount of ammonia is
$n(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 6.6\cdot 10^{11} / 17 = 3.88\cdot 10^{10}$ mol, so the
yield relation of R4 gives the fresh nitrogen feed
$n(\mathrm{N_2}) = 3.88\cdot 10^{10} / (2 \cdot 0.97) = 2\cdot 10^{10}$ mol,
the stoichiometric hydrogen demand
$n(\mathrm{H_2}) = 3 \cdot 2\cdot 10^{10} = 6\cdot 10^{10}$ mol, and the air
feed $n(\mathrm{O_2}) = 2\cdot 10^{10}/4 = 5\cdot 10^{9}$ mol.
Partial oxidation (R2) then consumes $2\,n(\mathrm{O_2}) = 1\cdot 10^{10}$
mol of $\mathrm{CH_4}$ and delivers $1\cdot 10^{10}$ mol CO and
$2\cdot 10^{10}$ mol $\mathrm{H_2}$.  Writing $x = n(\mathrm{CH_4,ref})$ for
the steam-reforming feed, R1 delivers $x$ mol CO and $3x$ mol
$\mathrm{H_2}$, and the water--gas shift (R3) converts all CO, contributing
a further $x + 1\cdot 10^{10}$ mol $\mathrm{H_2}$.  The hydrogen balance
reads $6\cdot 10^{10} = 2\cdot 10^{10} + 3x + (x + 1\cdot 10^{10})$, whence
$x = 7.5\cdot 10^{9}$ mol and
$n(\mathrm{CH_4}) = x + 1\cdot 10^{10} = 1.75\cdot 10^{10}$ mol; exactly,
eliminating the R1--R3 intermediates gives
$n(\mathrm{H_2}) = 4\,n(\mathrm{CH_4,ref}) + 6\,n(\mathrm{O_2})$, hence
$n(\mathrm{CH_4,ref}) = \tfrac{3}{8}\,n(\mathrm{N_2})$ and
$n(\mathrm{CH_4}) = \tfrac{3}{8}\,n(\mathrm{N_2}) +
\tfrac{1}{2}\,n(\mathrm{N_2}) = \tfrac{7}{8}\,n(\mathrm{N_2})$.
Finally
$m(\mathrm{CH_4}) = 1.75\cdot 10^{10} \cdot 0.016 \cdot 10^{-3}
= 280\,000$ tons; using the unrounded values,
$m(\mathrm{CH_4}) = 14\,n(\mathrm{N_2})/10^{6}
= 9\,240\,000 / 32.98 \approx 280\,170$ tons, which lies within $500$ tons
of $280\,000$.  In Lean, the bridge is closed by \texttt{linarith} from the
linear stoichiometric relations, and the mass bound is closed by
\texttt{linarith} via \texttt{abs\_le} after substituting the given data.
\end{proof}
