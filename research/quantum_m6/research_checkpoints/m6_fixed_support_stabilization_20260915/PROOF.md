## Claim

Fixed-support exact distance stabilization with an N-independent algorithm. Fix nonzero a,b in GF(2)[x] with constant terms 1, equal support weights, and restrict to orders N for which gcd(N,supp(a),supp(b))=1. Define G=gcd(a,b), a0=a/G, b0=b/G, R=max(deg a,deg b), r=max(deg a0,deg b0), D=wt(a0)+wt(b0), L=R(D-1), and K=L-r. Fix nonconstant F. For every N>RD satisfying gcd(G,x^N+1)=F, the associated two-block CSS code has d_X(N)=d_Z(N)=d(N)=min_{deg c<=K, F∤c}(wt(a0c)+wt(b0c))=min_{deg c<=L, F∤c}(wt(a0c)+wt(b0c))<=D. A fully specified dynamic program computes this constant and a logical witness using resources independent of N. The theorem includes even N and repeated factors. The accepted M5 repaired supports give infinite qualifying progressions. Explicitly, supports {0,3} and {0,1} have distance 4 at every N>12. This is a scoped symbolic M6 result, not an all-support classification or a claim that fresh independent reviews have been completed.

## Argument (verbatim)

1. Integration and evidence boundary.
Both supplied drafts and all five selected source manuscripts were read in full. The matrix constructor was inspected read-only. The first draft supplies the distance theorem with the stronger cutoff K=L-r; the second supplies an optimizer valid for every nonnegative cutoff. Substituting K into that optimizer closes their complementary mathematical gaps without changing its recurrence. The resulting proof follows below. No numerical catalogue entry, executed computation, AI vote, SELF lemma, or module shear is a premise. The C105 catalogue's withdrawal of earlier circular lower certificates is respected. Fresh independent reviews of this integrated composition have not occurred.

2. Conventions and cyclic boundary membership.
Let M=x^N+1 and S=GF(2)[x]/(M). Let A have row i supported at i+supp(a), and B have row i supported at i+supp(b), modulo N. The inspected constructor gives H_X=[A|B] and H_Z=[B^T|A^T]. Encoding a physical vector by (u,v) in S², the coefficient of x^i in bu+av is its H_Z syndrome at check i. Thus X cycles satisfy bu+av=0, and X boundaries are (ah,bh), h in S. Their weight is the sum of the weights of the degree-less-than-N representatives.
Choose ordinary p,q with pa0+qb0=1. The map phi(c)=(a0c,b0c) from S to S² is injective, since applying (p,q) recovers c. Its image consists of cycles. Moreover phi(c) is a boundary exactly when phi(c)=phi(Gh), equivalently c=Gh in S. For any ordinary representative C, this is equivalent to F|C: the forward implication follows from F|G,M; conversely, an ordinary Bezout identity F=alpha G+beta M, multiplied by C/F, expresses C in the ideal (G,M). No cancellation in S occurs.
The choice c=1 therefore gives a nonboundary cycle of weight D. Its products have degree at most r<=R<N. Consequently d_X<=D. Since F is nonconstant and divides G, R>=1, r<=R, D>=2, and K=R(D-1)-r>=0. Both minimization domains contain c=1.

3. Component unwrapping.
Take a minimum nonboundary X cycle of weight t<=D. Form a graph on its occupied physical qubits, keeping their block labels. A left qubit at i contributes checks i+supp(b); a right qubit at i contributes checks i+supp(a). Join distinct vertices if they contribute to a common check. Because N>R, a single vertex has no duplicate syndrome contributions. All vertices contributing to any check lie in one component. Their total contribution is even, so each component separately has zero syndrome. At least one component is nonboundary, since boundaries form a linear subspace. Let its size be s<=t.
Choose a spanning tree. For every tree edge from u to v, select a shared check, giving i_u+alpha=i_v+beta modulo N with alpha,beta in [0,R]. Lift the root to an integer congruent to its cyclic position, then set q_v=q_u+alpha-beta along each tree edge. Every lifted coordinate has the correct residue. Each tree displacement has magnitude at most R, and the path between any two vertices has at most s-1 edges. Hence max q_v-min q_v<=R(s-1).
Subtract the minimum lifted coordinate from all vertices, and form ordinary polynomials U,V from the two blocks. Distinct vertices in the same block cannot acquire the same integer position, since their original residues differ. Opposite-block vertices remain distinct physical qubits even at equal positions. Thus wt(U)+wt(V)=s and max(deg U,deg V)<=R(s-1). Modulo M the lift is precisely a common translation of the original component. Common translation is invertible and preserves both cycle and boundary spaces, so the lift remains nonboundary.
The ordinary polynomial Q=bU+aV has support in [0,Rs] and reduces to zero modulo M. Since N>RD>=Rs, its degree is less than N if it is nonzero. A nonzero polynomial of degree less than N cannot be divisible by M; therefore Q=0. This establishes unwrapping without assuming any consistency for non-tree edges. The strict inequality N>Rs is used explicitly.

4. Both distance inequalities and the cutoff.
In the ordinary integral domain GF(2)[x], Q=0 implies b0U+a0V=0 after cancelling G. Coprimality gives U=a0c and V=b0c for an ordinary nonzero c: a0 divides b0U and hence U, and substitution gives V=b0c. Degrees of nonzero products add, so deg c+r=max(deg U,deg V)<=R(s-1). Thus deg c<=K. The cyclic boundary criterion and nonboundary status give F∤c.
Write delta_K and delta_L for the two displayed finite minima. This lifted component proves delta_K<=s<=d_X. Conversely, every candidate with deg c<=L has output degrees at most L+r<=RD<N. Its ordinary products therefore embed without wraparound or loss of weight, and F∤c proves nonboundary status. Hence d_X<=delta_L. Since K<=L, delta_L<=delta_K. Combining gives delta_K=d_X=delta_L<=D.

5. X/Z equality and the quantum metric.
Let J map physical coordinate (block,i) to (opposite block,-i). It is an involutive weight-preserving permutation. It sends row i of H_X to row -i of H_Z, hence maps row(H_X) onto row(H_Z). Orthogonality of a permutation matrix then maps ker(H_Z) onto ker(H_X). Therefore J bijects X and Z nonboundary cycles, proving d_X=d_Z. A nontrivial CSS logical Pauli has at least one nonboundary X or Z component, and its physical support contains that component's support. A pure logical attains the smaller type distance. Thus d=min(d_X,d_Z), as claimed.

6. Multiplicities and the F=1 boundary.
The proof used ordinary Bezout identities, coefficient support, and polynomial division. It did not use squarefree factorization or a field decomposition of S. In particular, (G,M)=(F) keeps complete multiplicities for even N.
If F=1, G is invertible in S. Every cycle then satisfies b0u+a0v=0. With pa0+qb0=1, set c=pu+qv. The cycle equation directly gives a0c=u and b0c=v. Since c=Gh for some h, the cycle is a boundary. The J correspondence gives the same result for Z. There is no nontrivial logical distance in this case; it is excluded from the theorem.

7. Infinite qualifying orders and admissible examples.
More generally, whenever fixed supports have combined integer support gcd 1 and gcd(G,x^T+1)=F, let E be the multiplicative order of x in the finite unit group of GF(2)[x]/G. It exists because G(0)=1. For every j>=0 and N=T+jE, x^N+1 is congruent to x^T+1 modulo G, giving gcd(G,x^N+1)=F with all multiplicities. Infinitely many such N exceed RD, and the integer support gcd condition implies connectivity at each of them.
The accepted M5 Section 5 supplies exactly these fixed supports. Its invoked repair can be checked directly: pack a feasible period pattern into distinct exponents with the same residues modulo T; choose a positive exponent e in one block and let delta be the gcd of the other positive exponents. Feasibility gives gcd(T,delta,e)=1. For each prime p|delta with p∤T, choose k avoiding -e/T modulo p; if p|T, e is already nonzero modulo p. The Chinese remainder theorem gives a simultaneous k, and adding sufficiently many multiples of rad(delta) makes e+kT exceed every old exponent. Replacing e preserves residues and support weight, avoids collisions, and makes the combined integer gcd 1. The unchanged residues give gcd(G,x^T+1)=F. The preceding finite-ring argument proves the infinite progression; no other M5 claim is needed here.
For a=1+x^3 and b=1+x, G=F=1+x, a0=1+x+x², b0=1, R=3, r=2, D=4 and K=7. Every N has this complete signature, and the supports are connected and have equal weight two. F∤c means c(1)=1. Both c and (1+x+x²)c consequently have positive odd weights. A total below four would force both to be monomials; a monomial c makes the other product have weight three, a contradiction. The choice c=1 attains four. Therefore d(N)=4 for every N>12, including even N.
For a repeated-factor example take a=b=(1+x)^3=1+x+x²+x³. These are connected weight-four supports, with a0=b0=1, R=3, D=2. Every N divisible by four has F=(1+x)^3 because the multiplicity of 1+x in x^N+1 is at least four. The finite minimum is two, attained by c=1. Hence d(N)=2 for every N>6 divisible by four.

8. Exact dynamic program at the sharper cutoff.
The algorithm receives ordinary a0,b0,F,K; N is absent. Put f=deg F>=1, T_alg=K+r+1, and S_alg=2^(r+f). Write a0=sum A_j x^j and b0=sum B_j x^j, zero-padded through degree r. Process message bits t_i from low to high degree, with t_i=0 for i<0 and forced t_i=0 for i>K.
Represent residues modulo F by their f coefficients. Write F=x^f+sum_{j=0}^{f-1}F_j x^j. Multiplication by x modulo F is the explicit map X_F(q)_0=q_{f-1}F_0 and X_F(q)_j=q_{j-1} XOR q_{f-1}F_j for 1<=j<f. Initialize q_0=1 and update q_{i+1}=X_F(q_i), so q_i=x^i mod F.
At layer i, a state consists of memory m=(t_{i-1},...,t_{i-r}) and remainder rho=sum_{j<i}t_j x^j mod F. For r=0 the memory is empty. Let V_i(m,rho) be the minimum cost already charged at output positions 0,...,i-1. Initialize V_0(0^r,0)=0 and all other entries to infinity.
For i=0,...,K+r, allow t in {0,1} if i<=K, and only t=0 otherwise. From every reachable state compute u=A_0t XOR sum_{j=1}^r A_jm_j and v=B_0t XOR sum_{j=1}^r B_jm_j, with the sums taken over GF(2). Set rho'=rho XOR t q_i and, for r>0, m'=(t,m_1,...,m_{r-1}); for r=0 set m' empty. Relax V_{i+1}(m',rho') to the minimum of its current value and V_i(m,rho)+integer(u)+integer(v). Initialize each new layer to infinity. After the layer, update q_i by X_F.
The last r transitions are forced zeros: they charge all remaining product coefficients and flush memory to zero. Return the minimum V_{T_alg}(0^r,rho) over rho!=0. For reproducibility, enumerate states lexicographically in their listed binary coordinates, bits in order 0 then 1, and replace an entry only on strict cost improvement. Store its predecessor state and chosen bit whenever replacing it. Select the first minimizing terminal state, backtrack through every layer, discard the final r forced bits, and return c=sum_{i=0}^K t_i x^i. Ordinary XOR convolution reconstructs both output supports, which embed as a logical witness at every qualifying N.

9. Algorithm correctness and resource bounds.
Induction proves the stated memory and remainder invariants and q_i=x^i mod F. The charged bits u,v are exactly the coefficients at x^i of a0c,b0c. Future input bits cannot change an already charged coefficient. The products have degree at most K+r, so every output coefficient is charged exactly once. At a fixed layer, two prefixes with the same memory and remainder have identical future convolution costs and terminal acceptance under any common suffix. Retaining only the cheaper prefix therefore loses no optimum. Alternatively, induction on the recurrence proves that every state stores exactly its least prefix cost.
After flushing, rho=c mod F. Thus terminal rho!=0 is precisely F∤c, including multiplicities. Complete allowed bit strings enumerate all polynomials of degree at most K, and c=1 supplies a finite accepted path. The returned value is exactly delta_K; predecessor reconstruction attains it. This proves the optimizer's interface required by Section 4.
There are S_alg states per layer and at most 2T_alg S_alg transition examinations. In an indexed-array RAM implementation, explicit bit-vector arithmetic and costs bounded by 2T_alg give O(T_alg S_alg(r+f+log(T_alg+2))) work. Two-layer value storage uses O(S_alg(r+f+log(T_alg+2))) bits; storing all predecessors uses O(T_alg S_alg(r+f+log(T_alg+2))) bits. Backtracking and ordinary convolution fit within these conservative bounds. All parameters depend only on the fixed supports and F, not on N. Exponential dependence on r+f is explicit.
When r=0 there is no memory or flushing and the optimum is two. When K=0 the only accepted message is c=1, and the optimum is D. Repeated F is retained literally: for F=(x+1)^2, c=x+1 has nonzero remainder despite divisibility by rad(F). Replacing F by its radical would be incorrect. An optional F=1 algorithm input returns no feasible message; this is not an infinite-distance quantum-code assertion.

10. Scope and falsifiers.
The exact recipe group remains independent translations, a common unit multiplier, and block exchange. This theorem uses a fixed literal ordinary-support presentation; its cutoff need not be invariant under changing that presentation. No recipe-class count is identified with quantum-code inequivalence. It asserts neither stabilization at N<=RD, equality with D in general, nor a constant shared by different supports.
Concrete falsifiers are a qualifying N>RD whose exact distance differs from delta_K; a nonboundary syndrome component whose stated lift loses physical weight or fails ordinary zero syndrome; failure of the boundary/F-divisibility criterion with multiplicities; or a polynomial input for which the stated dynamic program disagrees with exhaustive ordinary multiplication. No such computation was performed or claimed. The symbolic composition closes the two drafts' mathematical interface gaps. Independent review and executable validation remain separate work.
