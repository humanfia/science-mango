# M7 reviewed module, not full milestone acceptance

Claim and argument reproduced verbatim from call-004 in run-efc8r824. Subsequent dual reviews found no gap in this module; review votes are not proof premises.

## Claim

For every N>=1, 1<=w<=N, and explicitly specified set S of distinct monic divisors of M=x^N+1 over GF(2), the algorithm below terminates and returns exactly one canonical representative of each connected weight-w recipe orbit having a member with complete signature in S. The group is precisely independent translations, one common unit multiplier, and block exchange. Each output includes a sector-realizing member, invertible action witnesses, and its entire deduplicated anchored orbit. Completion counts select exactly one leaf per newly emitted class; no Cartesian support-pair scan or traversal of all positive leaves is used. Full orbit expansion can nevertheless materialize every valid anchored pair. This proves the assigned generation sublemma, including even orders and w=1, not original M7 optimization or its fresh dual-review gate.

## Argument

1. Domain and change from the sources.
Let X consist of ordered pairs (A,B) of w-element subsets of Z/NZ with 0 in both supports and gcd(N,A,B)=1. Write a=sum_{i in A}x^i, b=sum_{i in B}x^i, and f(A,B)=gcd(a,b,M), monic with full multiplicities. Let X_S={y in X:f(y) in S}. An exact-F query means orbit intersection with X_{ {F} }, not a test on the canonical representative. An invariant signature query is a special case. S may be empty; all divisors may be selected. A finite decidable predicate on divisors can be converted to S by enumerating divisors, without enumerating supports.

M5 §§2–3 supplies exact prefix counts and recovery of a single realization. Its suggestion to traverse all positive branches does not establish one descent per class. The new ingredient here is a deduplicated full-orbit ledger and its exact subtraction invariant. No global birth bound, SELF lemma, finite catalogue label, or M6 distance assertion is used. The historical catalogue is read only to delimit its finite scope. The quotient audit supplies explicit group-normal-form and anchor arguments; these arguments, rather than its numerical experiments or verdict, are checked below.

2. All-order quotient and anchored orbit expansion.
Denote translations by T_{s,t}, the common multiplier by M_u, and exchange by E. Direct substitution gives M_u T_{s,t}=T_{us,ut}M_u, E T_{s,t}=T_{t,s}E, and EM_u=M_uE. Thus every group word has the form T_{s,t}E^epsilon M_u. These identities hold for every N. For N=1 use the single identity automorphism of Z/1Z.

For a nonempty support C, its least translated increasing tuple begins with zero: some translate does, and every such tuple precedes every tuple beginning positively. Therefore nu(C)=min_{c in C} sorted(C-c). Independent translation minima decouple in the lexicographic ordered-pair minimum. Consequently kappa(A,B)=min_{u,epsilon}(nu(uA_epsilon),nu(uB_epsilon)) is the minimum of the full orbit. It is constant on orbits; equal minima supply invertible actions taking both inputs to the same pair and hence prove orbit equality. Recording a minimizing unit, exchange bit, and anchor choices supplies a forward witness and its inverse. No polynomial square-freeness is involved.

For an anchored pair y=(A,B), define O(y) to be the deduplicated set
{(sorted(u(A_epsilon-alpha)), sorted(u(B_epsilon-beta))): u in U(N), epsilon in {0,1}, alpha in A_epsilon, beta in B_epsilon}.
It has at most L=2 phi(N)w^2 members. Every displayed member belongs to the orbit and is anchored. Conversely, an anchored image (uA_epsilon+s,uB_epsilon+t) contains zero in each block, so s=-u alpha and t=-u beta for some support elements alpha,beta. Hence O(y)=G_N y intersect {anchored pairs}. Store one generating action per distinct member; different actions may produce the same member and must not increase its count.

Connectivity is invariant: the subgroup generated by within-block support differences is unchanged by translations and exchange and is carried isomorphically by a unit. For anchored supports this subgroup is generated by A union B, so its being the whole cyclic group is exactly the stated gcd test. Thus every member of O(y) is in X. This also checks the all-order domain needed for the expansion.

3. Literal signature transport, including multiplicities.
In R=GF(2)[x]/M, substitution sigma_u(h)=h(x^u) mod M is an automorphism: substitution by an inverse of u modulo N is its inverse. The ideal generated by a,b is the ideal generated by their complete gcd F with M, by the polynomial Bezout identity. Translation multiplies each generator by a unit and exchange leaves this ideal unchanged. Consequently the signature after a common unit is exactly
 tau_u(F)=gcd(F(x^u),M),
where reduction modulo M before computing the gcd gives the same answer. This is a complete polynomial identity, not merely a statement about roots or degrees. It proves tau_v(tau_u(F))=tau_{vu}(F) and shows that tau_u permutes the monic divisors of M.

More explicitly, factor M=product_i p_i^{e_i}. The ideal (p_i) in R is maximal because R/(p_i) is a field. An automorphism permutes these maximal ideals and all their powers. The chain (p_i)^j stabilizes precisely at j=e_i, so the transported component has the same cap e_i. The field quotients also have equal size and hence equal polynomial degree. Products of these ideal powers represent every divisor ideal uniquely. Therefore tau_u transports each factor and its full multiplicity, preserving deg F. This reasoning includes nilpotents at even N. It does not infer a distance label or require square-free signatures.

For implementation and certificate replay, compute each stored member's signature directly as gcd(a,b,M), and check it against tau_u(F) using its action witness. A canonical representative may lie outside S. Keep its actual signature, the selected member's signature in S, and the action connecting them. Filtering canonical representatives by literal membership in S is expressly forbidden.

4. Exact counts at a prefix.
Use m=2(N-1) binary decisions: positions 1,...,N-1 of A, followed by those of B. At a prefix p, let A_0,B_0 contain the selected positions, including zero; let W_A,W_B be the undecided positions. Set k_A=w-|A_0|, k_B=w-|B_0|, z_A=sum_{s in A_0}x^s, and z_B similarly. Negative or excessive required weights give zero counts.

For monic P with constant term one, D=deg P, and W a finite set of positions, define
 n(P,W,k,z)=2^{-D} sum_{lambda in GF(2)^D} (-1)^{lambda dot (z mod P)} [t^k] product_{s in W}(1+t(-1)^{lambda dot (x^s mod P)}).
For P=1 there is one character and the answer is binom(|W|,k). Expanding the product chooses k-element subsets. Summing characters kills every nonzero residue sum and retains exactly the subsets whose sum equals z. This proves integrality and the exact interpretation. The coefficient is evaluated without subset enumeration as sum_j (-1)^j binom(n_-,j)binom(n_+,k-j), where n_- and n_+ count negative and positive character values.

For each F in S put
 C_F(p)=sum_{d | gcd(N,A_0,B_0)} mu(d) sum_{H | rad(M/F)} (-1)^{omega(H)} n(FH,W_A intersect dZ,k_A,z_A)n(FH,W_B intersect dZ,k_B,z_B).
All H in this sum are square-free divisors of rad(M/F). Define C(p)=sum_{F in S}C_F(p).

Here is an independent verification of the invoked M5 identity. For a completed anchored pair, summing mu(d) over divisors of N and all selected exponents is exactly the indicator of connectivity. For F dividing both polynomials, each factor p whose multiplicity in F is below its cap in M contributes exclusion of the event p^{v_p(F)+1} divides both. Expanding the product of these exclusion indicators gives the H sum; FH always divides M. These events test exact multiplicities, including partially saturated factors. The two character counts independently count block completions satisfying FH divisibility and the position restriction dZ. Thus C_F(p) counts exactly the members with signature F extending p. Distinct exact signatures are disjoint, so C(p)=|{y in X_S:y extends p}|. In particular C is nonnegative and C(p)=C(p0)+C(p1) at every nonterminal prefix. At a full leaf it is either zero or one.

5. Ledger algorithm and invariant.
Initially V is empty. Maintain V as an exact set of ordered anchored pairs, not a multiset of actions or hashes. Maintain a prefix trie counting only the members of V whose actual signatures lie in S. Define V_S(p) as that trie count and R(p)=C(p)-V_S(p).

Repeat the following deterministic procedure.
(a) If R(empty)=0, terminate.
(b) Otherwise start at the empty prefix. Evaluate both child residuals, choose the zero child when its residual is positive, otherwise choose the one child, and continue to a full leaf y. Keep V fixed during this descent.
(c) Compute c=kappa(y), record y, f(y), c, f(c), and an invertible action from y to c. Emit this class record.
(d) Generate every action in the displayed formula for O(y), deduplicate the resulting support pairs, attach signatures and action witnesses, and replace V by V union O(y). Insert newly stored members whose signatures lie in S into the counting trie. Restart at (a).

Inductively, after t emissions,
 V=disjoint union_{i=1}^t O(y_i),
and these are exactly the anchored members of the emitted orbits. At t=0 this holds. Assuming it holds, V intersect X_S is a subset of X_S, and therefore
 R(p)=|{y in X_S:y extends p and y not in V}|.
This proves nonnegativity and R(p)=R(p0)+R(p1), even though V also stores other sectors. A positive parent has a positive child. At the chosen full leaf R(y)=1, so y is valid and is not in V. If its orbit had already been emitted, the invariant would place y in V, a contradiction. Hence it is a new class, its entire anchored orbit is disjoint from the previous V, and the full expansion in step (d) proves the invariant for t+1.

Each descent removes at least its selected leaf from the uncovered set. The finite set X_S has size at most binom(N-1,w-1)^2, so termination follows without enumerating that bounding set. More precisely, the number of descents is exactly K, the number of recipe orbits meeting X_S.

6. Both directions of generation completeness and boundary cases.
Every emitted representative is in the orbit of its selected connected weight-w member with signature in S, so every output satisfies the stated orbit-intersection query. Representatives are distinct by the invariant and completeness of kappa. Conversely, every queried raw orbit contains an anchored member: translate each nonempty block by one of its elements. Translation preserves the signature. Such a member lies in X_S. At termination R(empty)=0 implies X_S is contained in V, so its orbit has been emitted. This proves both directions and certifies an empty output exactly when the initial C(empty) is zero.

For w=1, the only anchored pair is ({0},{0}); it is connected exactly at N=1 and its signature is 1. Thus there is one output precisely when N=1 and 1 belongs to S; otherwise none. Here m=0 is allowed: a positive root is already the selected leaf, its singleton anchored orbit is inserted, and the next root residual is zero. No logical distance, finite or infinite, is assigned. An empty S gives C=R=0. Invalid weights outside 1<=w<=N are rejected or explicitly reported as an empty support domain. Periodic supports and stabilizers cause repeated generating actions only; deduplication handles them. All proofs remain valid for even N.

7. Downstream interface.
Each record provides the canonical supports, their actual full signature, a connected sector-realizing anchored member, its signature in S, forward/inverse recipe actions, and the complete anchored orbit with per-member signatures and action witnesses. A downstream exact-F filter uses these realizing members, not the canonical signature alone. Multiple sector witnesses can be returned by filtering the stored orbit. Arbitrary translated raw members can be reconstructed by independent translations when requested. This interface deliberately distinguishes canonical classes from physical placements. It supplies neither exact distance nor locality labels. Those must be computed and certified by the separate selection module in its proved domain.

8. Costs and precise meaning of avoiding a raw scan.
Let r be the number of distinct irreducible factors of M, s=|S|, Q=binom(N-1,w-1)^2, K the number of emitted classes, L=2 phi(N)w^2, and E=sum_i |O(y_i)|. Then E<=min(Q,KL). Root and child tests require at most K(2m+1)+1 prefix-count evaluations. Exactly K completed support pairs are selected by count descent and canonicalized. There is no loop over the Q support-pair Cartesian product and no traversal of all positive leaves.

A conservative arithmetic bound for one count evaluation, using direct polynomial arithmetic and the character coefficient formula, is
 O(N^3 sum_{F in S} sum_{d|N} sum_{H|rad(M/F)} 2^{deg(FH)})
 <= O(N^3 s tau(N) 2^{r+N}).
This intentionally loose bound includes direct residue and character processing; it is not a bit-operation or efficiency claim. Counts and intermediate signed numerators have O(N+log(s tau(N)2^r)) bits up to polynomially bounded bookkeeping overhead, so elementary integer-operation bit costs must multiply this bound. Polynomial and integer factorization, divisor generation, and listing units are additional explicit preprocessing costs. They can terminate by exhaustive trial polynomial division and integer division, respectively, with potentially large cost but no support-pair enumeration.

Canonicalization costs O(phi(N)w^2 log(w+1)) residue operations per class by the anchor formula. Orbit expansion attempts at most KL action images, each costing O(w log(w+1)) residue operations. Deduplication can use an exact binary trie on the 2N-bit support encoding, giving O(KLN) additional work; computing and checking signatures adds O(KL G(N)), where G(N) is the cost of direct polynomial gcd and substitution arithmetic. A naive dense implementation has polynomial G(N). Filtering and inserting E distinct members into the prefix-count trie costs O(EN); its storage is O(EN) nodes, each with a count of O(N) bits, plus member, signature, and action records. Residual lookups can follow the trie during descent; using independent prefix lookups instead costs at most O(Km^2). Certificate output includes K class records and, if all member witnesses are requested, E orbit records. These are separate output costs.

Crucial limitation: for S equal to all divisors, V at termination equals all connected anchored pairs. Thus the ledger can store the entire connected anchored domain, and E can be of order Q. The construction does not prove avoidance of all pair materialization, smaller asymptotic work, or practical superiority. It avoids the raw candidate scan in the explicit task's sense: selecting one new representative by arithmetic counts and generating other members only through its known orbit. Counting every orbit image as a new candidate, or canonicalizing every positive leaf, would erase this distinction and is not this algorithm.

9. Replayable certificate and falsifiers.
A certificate specifies N,w,S, a complete factorization of M, the deterministic ordering conventions, each selected leaf and descent transcript, each canonicalization witness, every deduplicated anchored orbit with witnesses, and the final zero root residual. Counts may be recorded for convenience but are never trusted. A terminating verifier checks factor multiplication and irreducibility by polynomial division if necessary; recomputes all character and inclusion-exclusion counts; regenerates every orbit from all units, exchange bits, and anchor choices; compares exact sets; recomputes signatures and transport; checks canonical minima and action inverses; and rebuilds V and its prefix counts. At each descent it checks both child residuals, their nonnegativity and sum, and the selected positive branch. At each selected leaf it checks weight, connectivity, signature, and absence from prior V. Finally it recomputes C(empty)=|V intersect X_S|. The proved subset invariant turns that equality into coverage; a hash alone does not.

Replay uses the same finite arithmetic and orbit-size bounds, with transcript storage additionally O(Km) recorded count entries. It need not visit every unused tree node or enumerate the support-pair domain. Independent implementations may regenerate orbit closure differently, but no unperformed implementation check is claimed here.

Concrete rejection conditions are a counted duplicate action image, an omitted anchored orbit member, an invalid extra member, a wrong inverse action, a transported multiplicity mismatch, a selected canonical signature substituted for the realizing signature, a negative residual, a failed child partition, a repeated emitted class, or a claimed empty answer with positive final residual. Full orbit regeneration detects missing members even when the omitted member lies outside S. These checks and the invariant explain why numerical commitments alone are insufficient.

10. Original milestone boundary.
The assigned generation success criterion is met as a symbolic sublemma under the precise no-Cartesian-scan interpretation above, with its potentially exhaustive orbit-storage cost exposed. This repairs the generator-surjectivity gap left by M5's single-realization recovery. Original M7 still requires an integrated exact-label, feasibility, realizable-placement, all-optima and tie-preservation proof, followed by the requested fresh dual reviews of the unchanged integrated artifact. Neither those reviews nor an M6 interface audit was performed here. M5 is used only through the fully rederived §§2–3 count identity; any stronger M5/M6 dependency remains outside this claim. M8 efficiency is not imposed as a prerequisite.
