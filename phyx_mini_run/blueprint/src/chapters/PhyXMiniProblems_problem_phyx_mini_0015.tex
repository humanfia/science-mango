\#\# Physical scenario

A thief hides a precious jewel by placing it on the bottom of a public swimming pool. He places a circular raft on the surface of the water directly above and centered over the jewel as shown in figure. The surface of the water is calm.

\begin{theorem}[Physics formalization target]
\label{thm:physics:phyx_mini_0015:target}
\lean{PhyXMiniProblems.ProblemPhyXMini0015.problem_phyx_mini_0015}
\uses{def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-lengthquantity, def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-valueinmeters, def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-poolraftsetup, def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-jewelremainsunseenatdepth, def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-satisfieswateraircriticalanglelaw, def:physics:phyx-mini-0015:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0015-matchesanswertonearesthundredth}
For a centered opaque raft of diameter \(4.54\,\mathrm m\) over water of
index \(4/3\) in air of index one, there is a greatest depth at which every
escaping ray from the jewel is intercepted by the raft.  Its critical-ray
boundary satisfies \(h\tan\theta_c=d/2\), and the depth rounds to
\(2.00\,\mathrm m\), answer C.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The water--air critical law gives
\(\sin\theta_c=3/4\), hence \(\tan\theta_c=3/\sqrt7\) on the acute branch.
The limiting ray reaches the raft rim, so
\(h=(d/2)/\tan\theta_c=d\sqrt7/6\).  Escape-cone monotonicity shows this
depth is maximal, and certified bounds on \(\sqrt7\) give the stated
nearest-hundredth result.
\end{proof}
