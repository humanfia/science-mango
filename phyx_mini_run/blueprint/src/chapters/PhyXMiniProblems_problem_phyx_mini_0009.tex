\begin{theorem}[Physics formalization target]
\label{thm:physics:phyx_mini_0009:target}
\lean{PhyXMiniProblems.ProblemPhyXMini0009.smallestIncidenceAngle_is_choiceC}
\uses{def:physics:phyx-mini-0009:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0009-radiansofdegrees, def:physics:phyx-mini-0009:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0009-triangularglassprism, def:physics:phyx-mini-0009:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0009-canemerge, def:physics:phyx-mini-0009:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0009-answerchoicedegrees, def:physics:phyx-mini-0009:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0009-roundstonearesttenthdegree}
For a prism of apex angle $60^\circ$ and dimensionless index $3/2$ in air,
model both the principal-branch angles giving strict transmission into air
and the non-strict convention that counts the tangent critical ray as ``just
emerging.'' The strict set has infimum, and the non-strict set has least
element,
\[
\arcsin\!\left(\frac32\sin\!\left(60^\circ-
  \arcsin\!\left(\frac23\right)\right)\right),
\]
which rounds to $27.9^\circ$, answer C. The formal statement must distinguish
these two conventions rather than put the critical ray inside a strict set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the limiting ray the exit refraction angle is $90^\circ$, so Snell's law
gives the internal critical angle $c=\arcsin(2/3)$. The two internal
normal-referenced angles sum to the apex angle, hence the first refraction
angle is $60^\circ-c$. Entry Snell refraction then gives the displayed
inverse-sine expression. Monotonicity of sine on the principal branch shows
that smaller incidence cannot emerge and every larger admissible incidence
can. Thus the value is the strict set's infimum and, when the tangent ray is
admitted, the non-strict set's least member. Its degree value rounds to
$27.9$.
\end{proof}
