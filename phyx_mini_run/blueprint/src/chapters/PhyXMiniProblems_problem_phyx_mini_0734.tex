\#\# Physical scenario

Typical backyard ants often create a network of chemical trails for guidance. Extending outward from the nest, a trail branches (bifurcates) repeatedly, with \$60\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}circ\$ between the branches. If a roaming ant chances upon a trail, it can tell the way to the nest at any branch point: If it is moving away from the nest, it has two choices of path requiring a small turn in its travel direction, either \$30\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}circ\$ leftward or \$30\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}circ\$ rightward. If it is moving toward the nest, it has only one such choice. Figure shows a typical ant trail, with lettered straight sections of \$2.0\textbackslash{} cm\$ length and symmetric bifurcation of \$60\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}circ\$. Path \$v\$ is parallel to the \$y\$ axis.

\#\# Question
