\uses{def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-frequencyinhertz, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-axialvelocityinmeterspersecond, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-speedinmeterspersecond, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-sirenlistenersetup, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-matchesprimaryfigure, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-hassubsonicphysicalparameters, def:physics:phyx-mini-0195:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0195-satisfiesleftwardacousticdopplerlaw}
For a stationary siren and a listener moving away at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the heard frequency is the emitted frequency multiplied by $(c-30)/c$.  The unspecified sound speed and emitted frequency remain symbolic, so no displayed whole-hertz answer is selected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The figure gives listener velocity $-30$ and source velocity $0$ in the medium rest frame.  Substitute these two readouts into the leftward classical Doppler law $f_L=f_S(c+v_L)/(c+v_S)$ and simplify the numerator and denominator.
\end{proof}
