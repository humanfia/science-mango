\begin{theorem}[Physics formalization target]
\label{thm:physics:phyx_mini_0001:target}
\lean{PhyXMiniProblems.ProblemPhyXMini0001.thetaTwo_matches_choice_C}
\uses{def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-airwaterrefractionsetup, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-degrees, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-thetaone, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-thetatwo, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-haspositiverefractiveindices, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-satisfiessnellslaw, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-isphysicalrefractionangle, def:physics:phyx-mini-0001:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0001-matchesanswertonearesttenth}
For incidence from air into water with indices \(1\) and \(1.333\), incidence
angle \(45^\circ\), and the refracted angle on the physical acute branch,
Snell's law makes the refracted angle agree to the nearest tenth of a degree
with \(32.0^\circ\), answer C.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After inserting the two indices and the incident angle, Snell's law gives
\[
 \sin\theta_2=\frac{\sin45^\circ}{1.333}.
\]
The branch condition \(0\leq\theta_2\leq\pi/2\) makes sine injective and
identifies \(\theta_2\) with the corresponding inverse sine.  To prove the
display claim, compare this sine with the sines of \(31.95^\circ\) and
\(32.05^\circ\).  Monotonicity on the acute interval and certified bounds for
these fixed trigonometric values place \(\theta_2\) in that interval.  Scaling
by \(180/\pi>0\) yields the required \(0.05^\circ\) error bound.
\end{proof}
