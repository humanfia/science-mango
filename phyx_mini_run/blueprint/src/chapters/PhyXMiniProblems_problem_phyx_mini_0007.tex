\begin{theorem}[Physics formalization target]
\label{thm:physics:phyx_mini_0007:target}
\lean{PhyXMiniProblems.ProblemPhyXMini0007.incidenceAngle_eq_arctan_glassRefractiveIndex}
\uses{def:physics:phyx-mini-0007:phyxminiproblems-problemphyxmini0007-airglassraydiagram}
Let a ray pass from air of index one into glass of dimensionless index
$n_g>1$. Assume the reflected and refracted ray directions in the pictured
branch are perpendicular, reflection preserves the incidence angle, Snell's
law holds, and all normal-referenced angles are acute. Then the incidence
angle is $\arctan(n_g)$, answer C. Ray directions and the interface normal
must remain geometric objects rather than bare real numbers; real values are
only their named radian projections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\theta$ for the incidence and reflection angle and $r$ for the
refraction angle. Perpendicularity in the displayed branch gives
$\theta+r=\pi/2$. Snell's law therefore becomes
$\sin\theta=n_g\sin r=n_g\cos\theta$. Since $\theta$ is acute,
$\cos\theta>0$, so $\tan\theta=n_g$. The acute inverse branch yields
$\theta=\arctan(n_g)$.
\end{proof}
